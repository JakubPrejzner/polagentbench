We ask what post-training GGUF quantization does to agentic tool use in Polish, and whether the answer generalizes across models. We introduce PolAgentBench, a deterministic benchmark with Polish prompts and English tool schemas: a 67-task main suite (15 adversarial probes, 52 hard-tier tasks) and a 46-task arithmetic isolation ladder. Three models span two axes of variation: Bielik-11B-v3.0 and its pruned, distilled child Bielik-Minitron-7B-v3.0 isolate model compression, and Llama-PLLuM-8B adds a change of pretraining family. Each is measured at six precisions, Q8_0 to Q2_K. One result replicates and three do not. (1) The collapse threshold is shared: all three models fall off a cliff between 3-bit and 2-bit (11B 0.716 to 0.045, 7B 0.463 to 0.149, PLLuM 0.224 to 0.015; paired McNemar p <= 0.0002 in each), across a fourfold capability spread and both axes. (2) Failure modes do not replicate: at 2-bit the 7B keeps calling and never stops, the 11B answers at the first step with a confabulated final answer, and PLLuM fails on content while speaking the protocol fluently. (3) On the arithmetic ladder the unscaffolded rung is a floor of competence, confirmed by a rerun that states the no-tool rule; four explicit calls lift the 8-bit 11B from 1/10 to 9/10 and the 7B from 0/10 to 7/10 after an answer-typing artifact is corrected, an exploratory effect that does not survive multiplicity correction, while the order-trap arm separates the models (11B 6/6, 7B 0/6). (4) The Polish-versus-English gap behaves as a signature of degradation, not as a property of the interface. We document four benchmark artifacts that shaped our conclusions (rounding-hostile gold values, a priority-ordered failure taxonomy, strict answer typing, a no-tool rule the prompt never stated), report affected results in strict and corrected form, and release the benchmark, trajectories and commit-stamped artifacts.
