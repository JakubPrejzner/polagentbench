We ask what post-training GGUF quantization does to agentic tool use (calling the right tools in order and computing correct answers from their outputs) in a non-English setting, and whether the answer generalizes across models. We introduce PolAgentBench, a deterministic benchmark with Polish prompts and English tool schemas: a 67-task main suite (15 adversarial probes, 52 multi-step chains) and a 46-task arithmetic isolation ladder. Three models span two axes of variation: Bielik-11B-v3.0 and its structurally pruned, logit-distilled child Bielik-Minitron-7B-v3.0 isolate model compression, and Llama-PLLuM-8B adds a change of pretraining family. Each is measured at six precisions from Q8_0 to Q2_K. One result replicates and three do not. (1) The collapse threshold is shared: all three models fall off a cliff between 3-bit and 2-bit (11B 0.716 to 0.045, 7B 0.463 to 0.149, PLLuM 0.224 to 0.015; paired McNemar p <= 0.0001 in each), across a fourfold spread in capability and both axes of variation. (2) Failure modes do not replicate: the 7B hangs at the step budget, the 11B disintegrates in a single step, and PLLuM fails on content while speaking the protocol fluently. (3) On the arithmetic ladder, scaffolding helps: after correcting an answer-typing artifact, both Bieliks solve through four tool calls a computation neither solves bare (L0 0/10 in every run; up to 9/10 scaffolded, p = 0.004 and 0.016), while the order-trap arm separates them (11B 6/6, 7B 0/6). (4) The Polish-versus-English interface gap is a signature of degradation, not a property of the interface. We document three benchmark artifacts that shaped our conclusions (rounding-hostile gold values, a priority-ordered failure taxonomy, strict answer typing), report affected results in strict and corrected form, and release the benchmark, all trajectories, and commit-stamped artifacts.
